    What the taxman thinks land is worth

    BIR zonal values per square meter for Metro Manila and 8 nearby provinces. Tax floors, not market prices.

    BIR zonal values are being replaced. Under RA 12001 (2024), LGU market values approved by the Bureau of Local Government Finance become the single valuation base, fully by 2028 (GMA News). Until then zonal values apply, and this map shows them.

      How to read this

      Zonal values are BIR tax floors, not market prices. BIR uses them to compute taxes on property sales. Market prices usually run higher, sometimes by a lot. Use the map to see which places are valued above others.

      Interior lot is the lowest residential land value BIR sets in the barangay, usually for "all other streets". Prime is the highest, usually a main road or a named subdivision. Typical is the median of the barangay's street values, each street counted once. Condominium values are left out: BIR prices those per square meter of floor area, not land.

      Residential, commercial or industrial. BIR sets separate values for residential land (class RR), commercial land (CR, usually higher along main roads) and industrial land (I). The switch changes every view between them, including the villages, streets and condominiums shown when zoomed in.

      Zooming in shows BIR's values for individual villages and streets, matched to OpenStreetMap shapes. Up close, a barangay's color becomes its interior-lot value, what BIR charges on streets it does not list.

      Colored means the barangay has its own BIR values. Gray means it does not: no BIR schedule row matched it, or its city is not loaded yet. The map never fills a gap with a neighbor's or a city's value.

      Schedules are stale, unevenly. BIR revises each Revenue District Office on its own timetable, so a 2018 value is not comparable with a 2024 one. Projected to today grows each value from its schedule's effectivity date at its district's own pace: the median yearly increase BIR gave the same streets between consecutive revisions. It is a projection of BIR's own trend, not a market estimate; the card shows both numbers and the rate used. Data staleness (top right) colors the whole country by how old each schedule is.

      Color by swaps land value for another factor: share of the barangay in medium or high flood hazard (100-year rain), average elevation (a surface model, so towers add height), dry-season afternoon surface temperature (roofs and roads, not air temperature), and distance to the nearest rail station or expressway. Travel time estimates the drive from each barangay to a chosen business district, university, airport or regional center at three times of day. It runs on OpenStreetMap roads with a simple congestion model that has not yet been checked against real trips, so read it as a rough guide and use the range on each card. Jeepneys, buses and rail are not included yet. Condos marks buildings with BIR's condominium value, per square meter of floor area. Roads & rail shows expressways and rail, dashed where under construction and dotted where proposed.

      Before any transaction, verify the zonal value with the BIR Revenue District Office that covers the property. This site can be wrong or out of date. If you spot a value that is out of date, use Flag as out of date on its card.
