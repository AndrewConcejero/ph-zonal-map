      How to read this

      Zonal values are BIR tax floors, not market prices. BIR uses them to compute taxes on property sales. Market prices usually run higher, sometimes by a lot. Use the map to see which places are valued above others.

      Interior lot is the lowest residential land value BIR sets in the barangay, usually for "all other streets". Prime is the highest, usually a main road or a named subdivision. Typical is the median of the barangay's street values, each street counted once. Condominium values are left out: BIR prices those per square meter of floor area, not land.

      Solid colour means the barangay has its own BIR values. Hatched and faded means it does not: it shows the city median, or an estimate from neighbouring barangays. Grey means no data yet.

      Schedules are stale, unevenly. BIR revises each Revenue District Office on its own timetable, so most schedules in force date from 2018 to 2024. A 2017 value and a 2024 value are not comparable. "Adjusted to today" will project values forward using each district's own growth between revisions.

      Before any transaction, verify the zonal value with the BIR Revenue District Office that covers the property. This site can be wrong or out of date.
